The morning sun in Mysuru always seems to rise first at the Devaraja Market. 

Before the rest of the heritage city fully wakes, eighty-year-old Shivu is already at his stall, sitting cross-legged beneath a weathered wooden rafter that has stood for over a century. He carefully arranges his mounds of *kumkum*—vibrant towers of crimson, ochre, and deep vermillion powder—shaping them into perfect, smooth cones with practiced hands. 

Around him, the market breathes to life. It is a sensory symphony, unchanged since the days of the Maharajas. 

First comes the scent. The sweet, intoxicating aroma of Mysuru *malli* (jasmine) and orange marigolds, freshly plucked and woven into thick garlands. Then, the sharp, earthy fragrance of green bananas, betel leaves, and fresh coriander. 

"Banni, banni!" (*Come, come!*) calls a vegetable vendor nearby, his voice cutting through the cool morning air. 

By 8:00 AM, the narrow stone aisles are a kaleidoscope of motion. Sunlight filters through the high, arched ceilings, illuminating dust motes and the steam rising from tiny steel cups of hot filter coffee. Tourists with cameras dodge local grandmothers bargaining fiercely over the price of tender ridge gourd. 

A young girl stops at Shivu’s stall, touching the crimson powder with a curious finger. Shivu smiles, his wrinkled face folding into a map of memories. He remembers when his grandfather stood in this very spot, selling the same colors to the palace courtiers. 

Supermarkets have cropped up across the city, shiny and air-conditioned, but they lack a soul. Here, inside these historic brick walls, every transaction is a conversation, every scent is a memory, and every color tells the story of Mysuru. 

As the midday rush begins, Shivu looks out at the beautiful, chaotic dance. Devaraja Market is not just a place of trade; it is the beating, timeless heart of the city.
